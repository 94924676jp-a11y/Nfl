## Game reports
### CAR @ CLE
RotoWire predicts Bryce Young, Chuba Hubbard, Tetairoa McMillan, Jalen Coker, Xavier Legette and Tommy Tremble for Carolina; Deshaun Watson, Quinshon Judkins, Denzel Boston, KC Concepcion, Jerry Jeudy and Harold Fannin for Cleveland. These are **ROTOWIRE_PREDICTED**, never official starters.

McMillan is Carolina’s strongest receiving anchor, while Hubbard remains the primary back. Carolina reported AJ Dillon as the likely secondary back after Jonathon Brooks went to IR and cited Hubbard at 71% and 65% of snaps in the first two games.[^14][^15]

Coker and Legette are both questionable. If either is out, routes can move to the other receiver, depth WRs and additional TE packages; McMillan’s concentration rises probabilistically, but no player inherits all vacated opportunity.[^3]

For Cleveland, Watson’s rushing adds a real ceiling path and competes with Judkins near the goal line. Boston and Concepcion carry emerging rookie roles, Jeudy remains relevant depth/rotation, and Fannin can exploit Carolina’s confirmed linebacker/safety absences, though true route participation remains unverified.[^3]

**Correlation:** Young–McMillan expresses concentrated passing; Hubbard–CAR DST expresses a Carolina lead. **Outstanding:** official Coker/Legette status and wind.
### CIN @ PIT
Cincinnati’s observed role structure is concentrated around Burrow, Chase Brown, Chase and Higgins. Iosivas is out/IR, and Cincinnati described replacing its WR3 role by committee rather than assigning it automatically to one player.[^16][^2]

Brown’s workload is more sustainable than his short-sample efficiency; Chase and Higgins remain the dominant receiving paths. Colbie Young, Gesicki and other WR/TE packages can divide Iosivas’ routes.

Pittsburgh has Warren and Pittman questionable after limited work all week, while Dowdle is out. If Warren sits or is limited, reserve RB carries, Rodgers attempts and short targets all rise under broad uncertainty. **Correlation:** Burrow double-stacks are structurally supported by concentration. A Pittsburgh bring-back is useful for a sustained pass-volume script but unnecessary for a Cincinnati defense/short-field ceiling.
