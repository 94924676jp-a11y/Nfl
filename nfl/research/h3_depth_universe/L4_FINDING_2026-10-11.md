# L4: production's depth and appearance table is measured on a survivor universe (finding, 2026-10-11)

Status: **CONFIRMED AS A DEFECT IN THE INPUT; ITS EFFECT ON FORECAST QUALITY IS NOT YET ESTABLISHED.**
Research only. Nothing in production was changed. Found by the independent reviewer of H1; verified here in the
production code path.

## 1. The defect (measured, code-cited)

1. `player_prior.position_index()` (`nfl/tools/player_prior.py:248`) reads positions only from
   `nfl/vintage/weekly_rosters.*raw.csv.gz` (`:55`). Every roster row in the checkout is season 2026 (112,499 rows).
   So a player who left the league before 2026 has no position.
2. Share of panel players with a position, by season:

   | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 37.1 % | 50.1 % | 66.5 % | 79.2 % | 91.7 % | 100 % |

3. **Production** builds the depth table from that index: `pos_of = player_prior.position_index()` and
   `depth = depth_shares(panel, pos_of)` (`nfl/tools/proj_v1.py:1114-1118`). Inside `depth_shares`, players without
   the position are skipped, and the rank-r appearance rate is `n / n_club_weeks` (`proj_v1.py:1496`, `:1544`).
4. The allocator multiplies **every position's** claim by that rate: "APPEARANCE PROBABILITY FOR EVERY POSITION, not
   only quarterback" (`proj_v1.py:860-879`).
5. Club volume is counted from play rows, which include everyone. So a missing depth player's volume flows to the
   survivors at ranks 1-2.

**The table production uses (all history), roster-only against every player with a position:**

| rank | RB appearance | WR appearance | RB expected share | WR expected share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.000 / 1.000 | 1.000 / 1.000 | 0.492 / 0.563 | 0.259 / 0.277 |
| 2 | **0.635 / 0.972** | **0.861 / 1.000** | 0.110 / 0.199 | 0.126 / 0.174 |
| 3 | **0.230 / 0.563** | **0.587 / 0.985** | 0.012 / 0.037 | 0.050 / 0.102 |
| 4 | 0.030 / 0.103 | **0.266 / 0.803** | | 0.014 / 0.049 |
| 5 | 0.0004 / 0.007 | 0.077 / 0.463 | | 0.003 / 0.020 |

- QB rank 2: 0.138 / 0.190.
- TE is identical in both columns. Usage cannot tell a tight end from a receiver, so every inferred receiver is
  assigned WR (`forward_chain.positions_for_chain` docstring). The all-players WR column therefore contains some
  tight ends, and overstates WR depth somewhat.
- **The truth lies between the two columns.** Only historical rosters can measure it. They have already been
  requested as OUT-038, which needs network: the other agent's task.

**It reached a locked production artifact.** `nfl/dfs/salaries/DK_2026W4_EARLY_PROJ.json` carries WR3 appearance
0.562 and RB2 0.645 in its `depth_shares` block.

## 2. The repository already knew, and fixed it in only one of two places

- `forward_chain.positions_for_chain` (`nfl/tools/forward_chain.py:148-190`) documents exactly this defect: "the
  chain's universe silently became 'players who survived to 2026'".
- `forward_chain.evaluate()` (`:454-463`) applies that fix to the pool and the priors, but still passes the
  roster-only `pos_of` to `depth_shares`, `group_shares`, `bonus_rates`, `int_rate` and `positional_rates`.
- `nfl/eval/calibration.py:101-112` passes the season-consistent `season_pos` to all of them.
- **The repository's two evaluation harnesses are therefore inconsistent**, and production has neither fix.
- Fixing the harness is a change to validation tooling. It is proposed here, not made, and must not ride in the
  same commit as any production change.

## 4. A side effect found while measuring

**A production projection run rewrites a derived cache inside the repository.** `proj_v1.main()` opened
`nfl/derived/DST_RATES.json` for writing, via `dst_model`.

- The file is git-ignored and every production run regenerates it, so the shadow run did nothing a production run
  does not.
- But a research or replay run therefore **mutates a production cache**, from whatever play-by-play is on disk.
- The derived pin manifest (`nfl/production/DERIVED_REBUILD_MANIFEST*.json`) cannot hold across runs if this file
  can change underneath it: its hash today is `7a11e7fd…`, against the pinned `23108e2c…`.
- Recorded as a reproducibility defect. Not fixed: a fix would be a production change.

## 6. Portfolio effect, week-4 early Classic (appended after the rebuild; descriptive, one seen slate)

- **Inputs.** 163 of 378 players were rescaled, with factors from 0.77 to 4.90; the large factors belong to
  low-projection depth players.
- **Reproduction.** The incumbent rebuild reproduces the archived lineups 150/150, 20/20 and 3/3.

| contest | lineups shared | mean DK points, incumbent → depth fix | top 1 % entries | top 10 % entries | median field percentile (lower is better) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 150-entry | 11/150 | 129.41 → 126.99 | 4 → 7 | 39 → 35 | 0.301 → 0.310 |
| 20-entry | 3/20 | 132.33 → 133.73 | 1 → 2 | 5 → 3 | 0.303 → 0.268 |
| 3-entry | 0/3 | 157.13 → 154.99 | 0 → 1 | 2 → 1 | 0.044 → 0.159 |

**Reading it.**
- The depth table drives portfolio construction far more than the H1 candidate did (89/150 lineups shared there,
  11/150 here). Exposure moves from ranks 1-2 to depth players: MarShawn Lloyd +45 pp in the 20-entry; Elijah Moore
  and Ty Johnson +15-19 pp in the 150-entry.
- The outcome is **mixed**:
  - mean points are lower in two of three contests;
  - top-1 % finishes are higher in all three (4 → 7, 1 → 2, 0 → 1);
  - top-10 % finishes are lower in all three.
- One slate, already seen, with draws rescaled rather than re-simulated. It shows that **L4 is material to DFS
  decisions**. It does not show that the fix improves them. That is H3's prospective question.
