# Q8 — receiving opportunity and target allocation: decision

# REJECT

**The verdict is on the one repair the audit licensed. The audit itself is the
deliverable, and it is decisive.** Research only: nothing promoted, R8
untouched, no market input, no 2026 fitting, no RC2 repair arm reopened.

---

## Part 1 — the attribution audit

Forward-chained 2022–2025. 34,710 scored player-games, 22,521 in the
RC2-comparable population. **Exact Shapley over all 32 subsets** of the five
sources, using the repository's own `qb2_lib.shapley`. Every attribution adds
up to the total movement exactly.

| source | kind | receiving-yard **bias** | receiving-yard **CRPS** | player-target **CRPS** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **APPEAR** | realisation | **+2.118 (112.3 %)** | +0.009 (−3.3 %) | −0.006 (6.3 %) |
| **BUDGET** | realisation | −0.200 (−10.6 %) | **−0.299 (106.9 %)** | **−0.084 (91.5 %)** |
| SHRINK | parameter | −0.029 (−1.6 %) | +0.013 (−4.7 %) | −0.002 (1.8 %) |
| REDIST | parameter | −0.006 (−0.3 %) | −0.002 (0.6 %) | −0.000 (0.4 %) |
| CLASS | parameter | +0.003 (0.2 %) | −0.001 (0.4 %) | +0.000 (−0.0 %) |

**The three mechanisms the directive listed as candidates 2, 3 and 5 — the
class-level prior, the player-level shrinkage weight and the redistribution
rule — carry under 5 % of the movement on every value function.** They are
measurably not where the error is, and none was repaired.

### The headline finding: most of the bias is a selection artefact

The bias attributed to APPEAR is not a defect in R8's probabilities. It is
what happens when a **marginal** forecast is scored on a population **selected
by the outcome**. Same model, same draws, two populations:

| population | baseline receiving-yard bias |
|---|---|
| appeared players with prior history — RC2's and Q7's rule | **−1.998** |
| the whole union frame the model actually forecasts | **+0.139** |

Two yards of bias on one population, a seventh of a yard on the other.

This project's governing rule names the class: *"Define defects against
realised outcomes ... conditioning on the outcome manufactures fake defects for
any forecaster, including a perfect one."* A substantial part of the receiving
bias RC2 confirmed and Q7 localised upstream is exactly that. RC2 is not wrong
— it measured what it measured on a declared population — but **"fix the bias"
cannot mean fitting to the appeared-only number.**

### Answering the question as asked

*Why is unconditional receiving-yardage bias mostly generated by the
opportunity layer?* Because the opportunity layer is where appearance lives,
and appearance is the axis along which an outcome-selected population differs
from the forecast population. It is a property of the evaluation, not of the
target-share machinery.

### One thing I checked because it would have invented the result

The first version handed every draw the **same integer budget** — a point with
no spread, which under-disperses every player's targets and then hands the
whole inflation to the BUDGET oracle when removed. Rebuilt with the residual
pool P4B actually draws it with, BUDGET's share of target-CRPS movement went
96.9 % → 91.5 %. The ranking survives, for a stated reason.

## Part 2 — the one licensed repair

The audit leaves exactly one source both material and in bounds: the **team
target budget**, whose calibration slope is below 1 in every season. The
repair is one estimated parameter — `p' = m + β(p − m)`, β the OLS slope of
realised on predicted fitted on strictly earlier seasons, residual pool
recomputed around the new centre. Fitted β: **0.654, 0.606, 0.569, 0.566**.

### It does what it was aimed at

| | BASELINE | Q8_BUDGET_SHRINK |
|---|---|---|
| budget calibration slope | 0.575 | **0.954** |
| budget RMSE | 7.607 | **7.496** |
| budget CRPS | 4.272 | **4.200** (−1.67 %, CI excludes zero) |

### Downstream it splits, significantly, both ways

| target regime | n | ΔCRPS | significant |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | 17,507 | **+2.572 %** | yes, worse |
| `1-2` | 7,137 | **+1.398 %** | yes, worse |
| `3-5` | 5,687 | −0.643 % | yes, better |
| `6-9` | 3,289 | **−2.165 %** | yes, better |
| `10+` | 1,090 | −1.428 % | yes, better |

| appearance certainty | ΔCRPS | significant |
|---|---|---|
| near-certain | −0.212 % | yes, better |
| likely | +0.128 % | no |
| uncertain | +0.336 % | yes, worse |
| doubtful | +0.252 % | yes, worse |

**A better-calibrated team budget helps every player who actually gets targets
and hurts every player who gets none or almost none.** Overall player-target
CRPS moves −0.074 % with the interval spanning zero. Downstream receiving-yard
CRPS moves **−0.230 %** with the interval excluding zero.

Four strata significantly better, four significantly worse. The frozen rule
makes that REJECT, and it is REJECT.

## What else the run measured

**Replacement misallocation persists and is unchanged by this repair** — on
team-weeks whose starter was absent, the surviving starter is predicted 3.800
targets against 3.271 realised (**ratio 1.162**) while rotational and fringe
run 0.951 and 0.892. Q6 measured the same 19 % over-feed; the audit then
showed that correcting it is worth 0.4 % of the movement, which is why no
repair was aimed at it here.

**Starter dilution is mild**: 1.018 / 1.010 / 0.991 across the three classes.

**Zero-target probability** is under-predicted by 2.5 points (0.479 predicted
against 0.504 observed) and the repair does not move it (0.475).

**Target-share calibration slope is 1.077** with an intercept of −0.005 — the
share layer is close to calibrated in the mean, consistent with the audit's
finding that it is not where the error is.

**Player-target PIT χ² is 8,694** with 50 % intervals covering 0.760 — the
discrete low-count artefact `qb_v1.KNOWN_LIMITATIONS` already records for
quantities that are zero in most games, here on a population half of which
takes no target at all.

---

## What this says to do next

**Do not repair the class prior, the shrinkage weight, or the redistribution
rule.** All three are measured at under 5 % of the movement, two at under 1 %.
The badly-set shrinkage constant — fitted k of 0.75–0.84 against an
evaluation-optimal 4.0, a five-fold error — is worth 1.8 % of the target-CRPS
movement. Being wrong and mattering are different things.

**The budget repair is real but its downstream sign depends on target
volume.** If it is ever revisited, it should be evaluated on the population it
helps and gated by target volume, not applied globally — and that is a
different, larger change than the one this spec licensed.

**The appeared-only bias is not a repair target.** Any future work quoting
RC2's +2.18 should say which population it is on, because on the forecast
population the same model is biased by +0.139.

**Nothing is promoted.**
