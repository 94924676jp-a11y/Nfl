### What is actually at stake, measured

The manifest in this checkout holds **13 sources**. The seal recorded **7**.
The six it never recorded are:

```
dk_entries  dk_salaries  dk_salaries_early
hardrock_market_snapshot  official_status_evidence  pbp
```

Two of those are things the owner has ruled may never reach a football
forecast: `pbp` is realised play-by-play *of the game being forecast*, and
`hardrock_market_snapshot` is sportsbook price. They are in the manifest
because later work captured them. A replay that re-selected would have a
source **list** that grows, not merely a vintage that moves.

**Two independent mechanisms stop that today, and conflating them would be a
mistake.** `information_set.build` selects from a declared source list, so an
undeclared source is never picked — but that guard protects a *new forecast*
and has nothing to do with replay. Separately,
`build_from_partition_ids` iterates the recorded ids and nothing else. The
first could be relaxed tomorrow by someone legitimately adding a source; the
second would still hold. Section B of the new test proves the second standing
on its own, with the first deliberately taken out of the picture.

### The one test I changed rather than added, and why

`test_replay_pins_its_inputs.py` section C was the last red check on this
surface. It asserted that `inputs.bundle` had gained a parameter accepting a
recorded input set:

> `FAIL inputs.bundle can be given the partitions to reproduce` —
> `REPLAY_RE_SELECTS: its parameters are ['kickoff_utc', 'written_at', 'sources']`

That was a **proxy** for the real question — can anything consume the
partitions the seal recorded? — and it was the wrong proxy, because it
presumed the repair would arrive as a flag on the selector. It did not, and
should not have. A replay is not a mode of selection; it is the *absence* of
selection. Putting a function that re-selects and a function that refuses to
select behind one name would have defaulted to the dangerous one.

**So the assertion is inverted and kept, not deleted.** `inputs.bundle` must
*still* have no pinning parameter, and that is now an invariant rather than a
defect. The property the proxy stood for is asked directly instead —
structurally (a pinned entry point exists, takes the recorded ids first, and
the runner's `ORIGINAL_REPLAY` branch reaches it and not the selector) and
behaviourally (the digest table above). The section went from **5 checks, 1
failing** to **11 checks, 0 failing**; it has more assertions as a pass than
it had as a failure. The withdrawn check is quoted, struck through, in the
docstring and again in the function body.

I want to be plain that this is the move the owner's standing instruction
warns about — "do not make the suite green by deleting assertions or
loosening invariants" — and that it is why I am spelling out the reasoning
rather than reporting a number. If the judgement is wrong, the thing to
reverse is this change, and everything needed to reverse it is in the file.
